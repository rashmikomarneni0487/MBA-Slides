01 · The strategic challenge

How can CHQ extend the reach of its specialist expertise across Queensland while protecting the concentration of capability that quaternary excellence needs?

Together, three gaps create the core strategic challenge: how can CHQ extend the reach of its specialist expertise across Queensland while protecting the concentration of capability required for quaternary excellence? This is the question that drives our subsequent strategic analysis.

We identified three key gaps in CHQ's current position. First, there is a capability gap: demand for specialised paediatric expertise may grow faster than the supply of specialist clinicians. Second, there is a geographic gap: specialist capability is concentrated in Brisbane, creating access challenges for regional and remote communities. Third, there is an operating-model gap: a hospital-centric model alone may not be sufficient to achieve CHQ's broader statewide ambition.

Justification: each gap is anchored in CHQ's own data (Annual Report 2024-25, Master Plan 2021/22-2036/37). Presenting the gaps as evidence-backed, not assumed, sets up the analysis and the evaluation criteria that follow.

Five Forces shows the strongest pressure is supplier power: specialist clinicians are scarce and slow to train. Buyer power is also high because CHQ cannot direct other Hospital and Health Services. Rivalry and new entrants are low, so the challenge is not competition, it is capacity.

Internally, the VRIN and value-chain work shows CHQ's quaternary expertise and concentrated exposure to complex cases are valuable, rare, costly to imitate and hard to substitute. But the same workforce is asked to deliver complex care, train others and build statewide capability. Financially there is no buffer: surplus fell to $0.13m.

Justification: sources are CHQ Master Plan pp.11, 34; Annual Report 2024-25 pp.41-45; Porter (2008); Barney (1991).

MGTS7803 · UQ MBASource: Assessment 2 final options & evidence.

We developed three options that differ in how far they move from CHQ's current path, as the brief required. Option 1 extends the existing path: it scales shared care, specialist advice, outreach and local follow-up in two or three selected clinical pathways with willing partner HHSs. It builds on what CHQ already runs, such as Specialist Online Advice, ECHO, and the Rockhampton and Townsville partnerships. Option 2 is a radical change within the existing market: CHQ becomes the network orchestrator, jointly setting statewide pathways, standards, shared records and a quality dashboard. Option 3 expands into new markets: packaging education, research partnerships and intellectual property for interstate, private and international buyers.

Justification: the three options cover the three strategic directions in the task brief. Each tackles the gaps differently, Option 1 through pathways, Option 2 through the operating model, Option 3 through new revenue. Each has a distinctive main risk, which drove the evaluation.

We recommend Option 1, but we modified it in four ways to make it fit CHQ's constraints. First, the core is an ECHO-led workforce redesign. GPs, nurse practitioners, nurse navigators and advanced-practice allied health take on more triage and case management, backed by ECHO and consultant-endorsed protocols, so QCH specialists concentrate on quaternary care. This targets the capability gap directly. Second, it runs as a jointly governed program with partner HHSs. Third, we added a funded data foundations and AI assurance workstream, because our review found CHQ cannot yet measure networked care. Fourth, we made it a real option: capped spend, gates at 6, 12 and 18 months, and Option 2 deferred to a business case at month 18, while Option 3 goes to a six-month asset review with no revenue assumed.

Justification: the evidence is local. In CHQ's ADHD ECHO, GP self-efficacy rose from 2.6 to 5.4 out of 7; a GP-paediatrician trial cut high-referring GPs' referrals from 7.3 to 3.0 percent. Telehealth already runs 25 percent above target.

The recommendation creates value for the organisation and for each key stakeholder. For CHQ, the program is about $4.4 million over 18 months, 0.38 percent of annual expenditure and about 5 percent of the precinct capital a working network could avoid. The Master Plan's fully networked scenario cuts additional QCH beds needed by 2036/37 from 95.7 to 22.0, and precinct capital from $229.1 million to $134.2 million. We are not claiming Option 1 delivers all of that; it tests whether the larger commitment is worth making.

For families, less travel, faster follow-up and a named coordinator. For CHQ clinicians, protected time for complex care, teaching roles that support retention, at a time when engagement is 68 percent. For partner HHSs, new local capability and ABF retained locally. For the Department of Health, evidence for the Service Agreement and avoided capital. For Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander families, care on Country, building on Deadly Ears and IROC.

There are trade-offs. CHQ will lose some activity-based funding as follow-up moves locally. Our illustrative model shows about 80 percent is offset through telehealth funding and refilling freed slots, but this must be validated by CHQ Finance.

Implementation runs in four phases with Board decision gates. Phase 0, months 0 to 3, mobilises: appoint an executive sponsor and clinical lead, form the Joint Pathway Governance Group, confirm the three HHS partners, and complete baselines and a data-readiness assessment. Phase 1, months 3 to 6, is co-design with clinicians and families, writing eligibility, escalation and return-to-CHQ criteria, and confirming credentialing under the Clinical Services Capability Framework. Phase 2, months 6 to 15, launches the pathways in waves with protected specialist advice sessions, ECHO cases linked to each pathway, and a monthly dashboard. Phase 3, months 15 to 18, is an independent evaluation and the Board decision to scale, adapt or stop, with an Option 2 business case only if the data-readiness gate is met.

Justification: we followed Kotter's sequence. With 68 percent engagement and 14.2 percent vacancies, this change must be co-designed with clinicians, not imposed. The staged design limits clinical, financial and relationship risk.

The main risks are manageable. Clinical safety is controlled by CSCF-based eligibility, credentialing, 24/7 escalation through CATCH and stop rules. Rising specialist workload is addressed by funding protected sessions before any appointment moves. Partner capacity is handled through backfill and the right to decline. Data risk is addressed by Workstream 8 and a data-readiness gate before Option 2.

Our assumptions, including partner capacity, funding of advice time outside ABF, and the indicative budget, are stated openly for CHQ to validate.

09 · Conclusion
Protect

Keep complex, high-acuity and genuinely quaternary care concentrated at QCH.

Extend

Use networked care, ECHO®, shared-care pathways and local capability to bring appropriate care closer to families.

Build

Use the 18-month stage-gated pilot to establish evidence, governance and data foundations for a future network-orchestrator model.

Option 1 delivers value now — while building CHQ's capability to transform later.

MGTS7803 · UQ MBAChildren's Health Queensland

To conclude, CHQ's strategic challenge is not a choice between statewide reach and quaternary excellence. It is deciding what must remain concentrated at QCH and what can safely be delivered closer to home.

Our recommendation therefore has three elements. First, protect quaternary excellence by keeping the most complex and high-acuity care at QCH. Second, extend CHQ's specialist expertise through networked care, ECHO®, shared-care pathways and stronger local capability. Third, build the evidence, governance and data foundations through an 18-month stage-gated pilot.

This is why Option 1 delivers value now while preserving the option to transform later. At month 18, CHQ can make an evidence-based decision on whether the conditions are strong enough to progress toward the network-orchestrator model.
